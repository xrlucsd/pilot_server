Room Observation Task

Instructions

  • Ensure the subject is fitted with their microphone, AirPods, or headphones.
  • Press play on the introduction. The remaining audio advances automatically as each timed section finishes.
  • Each response period is timed, and recording starts and stops automatically.
  • The subject hears one beep when recording starts and two beeps when 15 seconds remain.
  • Use the individual audio controls only when a section needs to be replayed.
Start the introduction audio when the participant is ready.

Introduction and observation

Transcript: Welcome. Please spend 60 seconds observing the space around you. Pay attention to all the details you notice: the lighting, furniture, objects, and any other features of the room. Afterward, you will be asked a series of questions about the space. Now begin observing the room.

Not started.

Response instructions

Transcript: Now, you will answer a series of questions about the room. Feel free to look around the room as you answer. Speak your answers clearly. Please provide as much detail as possible within the given time for each question. Do your best to speak for the whole time. If you need time to think before you answer, please say your thought process aloud so that you are talking for the whole time. You will hear one beep after each prompt, indicating that you can begin talking. You will hear two beeps when you have 15 seconds remaining to finish your answer.

Not started.

Question 1

Transcript: Imagine describing this room to someone who cannot see it. Speak for two minutes and include as much detail as you can. Be detailed enough that someone could picture the space based on your description alone. You may begin now.

Not started.

Question 2

Transcript: Now, we would like to know how you would rearrange the space to best accommodate or support different types of activities, such as chatting over a cup of coffee with someone or doing independent work. How would you move the furniture? You won't actually move it; just describe each activity and the corresponding arrangement. You can also suggest multiple different arrangements for the same activity. Your rearrangement might make the space more functional relative to certain activities, or more aesthetic, efficient, or any other change you find important. Think of as many different arrangements as possible that could help facilitate different activities. You will have three minutes. You may begin now.

Not started.

Wait message
